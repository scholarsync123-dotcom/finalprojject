🎓 What is HCI?

Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) is a multidisciplinary field focusing on the design of computer technology and, in particular, the interaction between humans (users) and computers. It combines elements of psychology, cognitive science, design, and computer science.

Quantitative Interaction Laws

Empirical models predicting human performance in interaction tasks

📐 Fitts's Law (1954)

MT = a + b log₂(2D/W)
Movement Time (MT) is a function of distance (D) and target width (W). Index of Difficulty (ID) = log₂(2D/W) measured in bits.

⏱️ Hick's Law (1952)

RT = a + b log₂(n)
Reaction Time (RT) increases logarithmically with the number of choices (n). This informs menu design and decision architecture.

📈 Power Law of Practice (Snoddy, 1926)

T = aP⁻ᵇ + c
Task time (T) decreases as a power function of practice (P). Explains learning curves and expertise development.

🎨 Gestalt Principles (Wertheimer, 1923)

Humans perceive visual elements as organized patterns rather than individual components. Principles: Proximity, Similarity, Continuity, Closure, Figure-Ground.
